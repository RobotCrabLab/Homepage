Before I moved into building instruments, I spent more than eleven years at Moss Landing Marine Laboratories collecting and processing the samples those instruments are built for. I coordinated environmental sampling throughout California, assessed wetland habitats, and led sampling efforts as the go-to person for water chemistry instrumentation. Along the way I logged more than 200 hours running boats and kept the sampling gear and research vessels in working order.

## Life on a research vessel

On deck, a CTD rosette waits to go over the side. The sensors at its base measure conductivity (which gives salinity), temperature, and depth as it drops through the water column. The gray cylinders ringing the frame are sampling bottles that can be closed at chosen depths to bring water back up for analysis.

Just inside, a staging room holds survival suits, foul-weather gear, and the hoses and fittings that keep the deck running. Past that is the main lab, where samples get processed, logged, and stored before the ship comes back to port.

## From the field to the lab

Back on land, I helped manage an ISO 5 (Class 100) trace-metal clean laboratory, where even a speck of dust can throw off a measurement. I tracked the receipt of up to 6,000 samples a year, prepared them for analysis, and maintained the equipment and calibration logs. I also built an instructional program covering the essential sample collection and processing protocols, so new team members could learn them the same way in the field and in the lab.
